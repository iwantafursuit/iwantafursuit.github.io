How to spot problems in a fursuit commission

An unexpected payment message deserves a fresh check. Open the maker's official profile from a bookmark or the portfolio you already confirmed. Use that contact to verify the invoice and recipient, even if a similar-looking account says it is sending an urgent correction.

Check the portfolio in context

A single picture can be reposted without context. Look for multiple views of a finished piece, dated progress from the same account or a public credit that links back to the maker. Ask a specific construction question about the picture you are using as a reference. Unclear evidence can have an innocent explanation, so stop and ask for clarification before treating it as proof.

Take time over the payment terms

Ask for a written invoice or agreement that identifies what you are purchasing. Read the payment provider's current eligibility rules for custom goods, dispute deadlines and payment types before sending money. It is useful to know which dates matter before a long production window starts.

If a seller asks you to pay a new recipient, demands immediate payment to keep a slot or refuses to explain what the deposit covers, pause and verify through the contact channel you used before.

If something goes wrong

Save the terms, invoice, messages and receipts. Contact the maker via the agreed channel and ask for a response on the specific issue. If you suspect impersonation, contact the maker through a separately verified channel and consult the platform or payment provider's official reporting instructions. Public reports should leave out personal details and documents.
