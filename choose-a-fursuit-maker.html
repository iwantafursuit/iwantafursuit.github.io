How to choose a fursuit maker

Start with the pieces you need and one or two features you care about most. If you need a head you can wear with glasses, put that requirement beside your reference sheet. If you mainly plan to wear a partial for photos, ask how the head, paws and tail will work with your own clothing. Those requirements make a portfolio easier to assess.

Compare work that resembles your commission

Look for completed pieces with something in common with your character. For a striped character, look at how markings line up across seams. For a partial, look for a photo of the head and paws worn together. Ask for front and side views if the portfolio mostly shows one angle. A maker may have different construction options for the same appearance, so ask which example is closest to the one they are quoting.

Compare offers on the same terms

Ask both makersWhat to write down
Which parts and extras does the quote include?List each part and whether shoes, padding or accessories are separate.
How do we confirm fit before the build?Record the requested measurements, when they are due and whether changes cost extra.
How will I know when work begins?Ask for the planned start window and what counts as a progress update.
Who arranges shipping and repairs?Record who pays for shipping both ways and how the maker handles fit or construction issues.

If an offer looks cheaper, check the scope first. A quote without feet or padding can be useful, but compare it with the other offer only after confirming what you will receive.

What if a maker is closed?

Ask where they post openings and whether requests are being accepted for a waitlist. A waitlist can mean an invitation to request a quote later. Ask what securing a slot means and at what point money is due.
